Same strategy, same bars, same costs. The difference is entirely the
bar at which an order is assumed to fill. Nautilus's convention is the
optimistic one -- it acts on a close at that same close, which needs
the close before it prints.

SECOND DIVERGENCE: THE CASH CONSTRAINT
==========================================================================
sizing 759 shares off the FIRST close costs $266,090 at the sample high
  reference / quantdesk     187,960.50   cash may go negative
  vectorbt                  177,606.96   partially fills instead
  max divergence             11,775.96

VectorBT and Nautilus refuse to spend cash they do not have. quantdesk
does not model the constraint at all, so a backtest there can hold a
position it could never have funded, financed at zero cost. That is a
gap in quantdesk, not in VectorBT, and it is invisible until position
size approaches account size.

So: quantdesk and VectorBT are interchangeable and agree to floating
point WHEN THE CASH CONSTRAINT DOES NOT BIND. Nautilus in BAR mode is
not comparable to either; use it for execution and live, or feed it
quote/trade ticks so the fill is real rather than a convention.
